Short answer

Nothing they plan conflicts with the original design. Most of it moves the code towards the design, which the code does not yet match.

  • The trustless claim is the design's, not yet the code's. On the main branch today a member trusts the host in three places. The planned work removes all three.
  • Some planned parts are new design that the posts do not contain. They are reasonable, but they are theirs, and should be labelled and reviewed as such.
  • Two things narrow the design on purpose: one member per leaf, and liquidity sales switched off for now. The first costs flexibility for good. The second is temporary but means the "dynamic" part of the design does not work yet.
  • Four points strain the design's intention and need a decision by you, not by either AI. They are in section 6.

5. Round 2: the end-date payout

The design posts call this "inversion of the timeout default". At the end date the money goes to the members, not the host, so the host must close properly or lose it. This is the property the trustless claim rests on.

5c. Parts that narrow the design

PlannedWhat is lostVerdict
One member per leaf only (proposed for the end-date payout).The posts put two or more members on a leaf so the host can shift spare liquidity between them cheaply. With one member, spare liquidity can only go to that member; moving it elsewhere needs a round of the whole tree.
Correction, 2026-11-11: the shared leaf is already built. The tree builder makes K members per sub-factory sharing the spare liquidity, K sub-factories per leaf (factory_set_ps_subfactory_arity, factory_subfactory_chain_advance_unsigned), with a penalty for a stale chain and tests. It is off by default (K = 1) and switched off in use by #478 and #481. Gazpacho already imports this code. The SuperScalar session now plans to move the default towards K > 1, which conflicts with this row: see below.		Narrows A deliberate trade, now in tension with the shared leaf that exists: see below.
Round 2 lands in two steps: tree outputs first, channel outputs later.Between the steps the host keeps a timeout claim on channel outputs.Narrows Temporary. The caller-supplied script may remove the gap.
Why one member per leaf is the safe choice

The second post warns about this itself. With a shared leaf, a member who buys the spare liquidity and then receives payments can hold more than its payout covers. The host then gains by letting the payout fire. The post describes the protection as "limited coverage", like deposit insurance. With one member per leaf the payout equals the whole leaf, which the member's balance can never exceed, so the protection is complete. Their review found the shared-leaf case to be a real loss of member money and rated it a blocker for those shapes.

The tension, as of 2026-11-11. The shared leaf (K > 1) is built and is what the design posts describe. The end-date payout, as drafted, is only fully safe with one member per leaf. Both cannot be the default without one of the fixes the review lists: re-sign the payouts above a leaf whenever liquidity moves inside it (a round of the whole tree); or cap each member's balance at its covered amount until then; or keep K > 1 only before the end-date payout exists and accept the "limited coverage" the second post describes. This needs one decision shared by both projects.

6. Where the plan strains the design's intention

None of these breaks the design. Each is a place where what a member or host can be promised is weaker, or costlier, than a first reading of the posts suggests.

PointWhat it meansWho decides
A missed deadline is a race, not a forfeit.The posts say the host "risks loss of all funds". That is accurate: it is a risk. A late host can still publish and sometimes win. So members cannot be promised full capacity when the host is late, and members must keep watching after the end date.You: what members are told (decision B).
Members gain when the host fails.A member with a zero balance is paid its full capacity if the payout fires. The posts accept this because the host always has something to lose. With seats that cost nothing it invites stalling and mass silence.You: whether a seat costs something.
Offline members are the normal case on phones.Today an absent member costs the host nothing. After the change each absent member costs the host a forced path on chain at every rotation, with a hard deadline. The posts intend the host to pay; the size of the bill at hundreds of members has not been measured.You: decision E.
Rotation has a trust window either way.Old order: members briefly hold only the host's promise. New order: the host briefly owes in both factories and needs funds for both. The trustless choice is the new order, with the new funding confirmed before a member gives up its old key.You: decision D.
