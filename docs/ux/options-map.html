The six scopes

1. My conditionsWhat I will accept. Mine alone. The app joins and signs only inside them.
2. Lobby termsWhat a host offers. Set by the host, signed, numbered. A member can read them, never edit them.
3. WalletMy keys, my backup, my on-chain coins, the network.
4. WatchingWhat protects me while I am away, and how I am told about dates.
5. ConnectionsWhich outside services the app talks to, and whether they agree.
6. HostingEverything about being a host. Hidden until host mode is on.

The one relationship that matters most: scope 1 and scope 2 are two sides of one comparison. A lobby fits when every term (2) is inside every condition (1). The app shows the two side by side, row for row, and says which row fails. They must never be editable in the same place.

Never a setting

The specifications say the software decides these alone. No layout may offer a switch for them, and no "advanced" area either.

  • Every safe-to-sign check. A member cannot loosen them; conditions can only be stricter.
  • Saving before sending, and never reusing a one-time signing secret.
  • Comparing at least two chain servers and stopping when they disagree.
  • Answering an old state, and leaving before the factory ends.
  • Refusing mainnet in this release.
  • Signing once I am marked ready and the lobby starts. Readying is the consent; there is no prompt in the middle of a round.

Rules any layout must keep

  • Each option lives in exactly one scope. Scope 1 and scope 2 are shown together only as a comparison.
  • A new member can join safely by choosing profiles and nothing else.
  • The most urgent item is visible from every screen.
  • Every countdown says what happens at zero.
  • The pause control is reachable from every screen.
  • Whatever the app did alone is listed afterwards, with reasons for refusals.
  • Member and host live in one app without the member seeing host controls.
  • Nothing promises more than the specifications support. Open decisions are marked, not guessed.
  • Words: host, member, hosting, providing liquidity, fees. Never "staking".
