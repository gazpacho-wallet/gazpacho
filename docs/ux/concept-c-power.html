Set up Gazpacho

Four short steps. You can stop after any step and come back.

1 What this is2 Your wallet3 What you accept4 Find a lobby

1. What this is

Gazpacho is a Lightning wallet. You keep your own keys. Nobody else can spend your sats.

You share one on-chain output, called a factory, with a host and other members. The host pays for everything. You start with a balance of zero and a channel you can receive into.

You join through a lobby: a waiting room with fixed terms written by the host. You keep your own conditions. The app signs only when the terms are inside your conditions.

This is Signet. The coins are test coins with no value. Mainnet is not available in this release.
Next: your wallet

2. Your wallet

Create a new walletI already have a recovery phrase

A new wallet gives you 24 words. Write them on paper. The app will ask you to type some of them back before it continues.

The 24 words are half of your backup. They restore your keys. They do not restore your place in a factory. That needs the factory backup, which the app makes by itself after you join. Both are on the Wallet tab.

You need no on-chain coins to join. A small amount helps later if you ever leave a factory alone.

3. What you accept

Hosts label each lobby with a profile. Pick the profiles you accept. That is enough to join safely. Every other limit starts at a sensible value and can be changed later on My conditions.

     

Automatic joining is off. You choose each lobby yourself until you turn it on.

Next: find a lobby

4. Find a lobby

Nothing works until you are in a live factory. You cannot pay or receive yet. A lobby starts at its own time, usually within hours. After it starts and confirms, your channel is live.

3 of the 14 listed lobbies fit what you accept. The soonest starts in 2 h.

About

Gazpacho 0.1.0, Signet build.

About this concept

Concept C treats Gazpacho as a desktop program for someone who wants to check the work. Every area has one tab. Every table sorts. Every date says where it comes from. Every transaction and signature can be opened. The same shape serves hosting, which needs all of it: anyone can host, and the Hosting tabs are always there.

One deliberate break from the models: Sparrow has no dashboard and Electrum keeps the balance in the status bar. The research lists the second as a pattern to avoid, so this concept adds an Overview tab with "my money" first.

Strengths

  • One obvious home for each scope. Nothing sits in a general settings list.
  • Trust by inspection. A member can see the tree, the path out, each held transaction, each signature and the arithmetic behind each date.
  • Terms and conditions really are side by side, row for row, because a desktop pane has the width for it.
  • The status bar answers "am I connected, is something watching, what is next, will the app act alone" on every screen without a click.
  • It scales to hosting without a second product or a mode: more tabs below a divider, the same tables.
  • Terms are inspectable down to the rules fingerprint, and the fit check compares whole ranges, so a member can see why a last-moment change is impossible.

Weaknesses

  • Everything visible at once. This is the known fault of the style; the research records Sparrow rated 6 of 10 for ease. Eight tabs, plus up to six for hosting, is at the edge of what people scan.
  • It reads as a tool. A first-time member who only wants to receive sats meets tables, blocks and sub-tabs.
  • At phone width it survives but is not good: the status bar shrinks to short labels, tables become stacks of labelled rows, and the side-by-side fit check becomes one long column.
  • Two view levels mean a member in Everyday view may not know the Tree tab exists. A hint line says so, which is a patch.
  • Sub-tabs inside tabs put some screens two levels down, the limit the research recommends.

How the fault is limited here

  • Overview comes first and holds only three things: my money, my factory's next date, and what the app will do alone.
  • Everyday view is the default. Tree, Transactions and five rarer limits appear only in Inspect view. No option is hidden for good, and there is no secret door.
  • One detail pane at a time, under its table. Sub-tabs instead of long pages.
  • The urgent strip shows one item, never a list. The list is one click away, in tiers.
  • The status bar has five fixed segments and a button, in words.
  • A new member can join by ticking profiles and nothing else.

What I would change after seeing it

  • Use this as the desktop and host form only, with a simpler member home in front of it on a phone.
  • Fold Watching into Factories as a sub-tab. Its real content is dates, and the dates already live there.
  • Make table rows open the detail in a pane to the right at widths above 1,400 px, as Sparrow does, instead of below.
  • Test whether "Everyday / Inspect" is understood, or whether per-tab "show more" links are clearer.
  • Version 1.1.0 screens (buying receiving room) are behind "Preview 1.1.0" in the Moments menu and tagged 1.1.0; in 1.0.0 one line under "can receive" stands in for them.
  • Not built here: changing a limit does not relabel a set of profiles as "Custom", because profiles in this map are labels on lobbies, not presets of my limits. That needs an owner decision.
