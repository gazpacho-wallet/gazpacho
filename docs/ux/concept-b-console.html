Welcome to Gazpacho

A Lightning wallet where you hold your own keys. You share one on-chain output, a factory, with other members. A host pays for it. You start with nothing in it and can receive at once.

Nothing works until you are in a live factory.You cannot pay or receive over Lightning before that. Getting in takes four steps. Only the last one waits for other people.
Signet: test coins, no value.This release refuses the main Bitcoin network. That is not a setting.
  1. 1. Make a wallet, or restore one
    Done: new wallet made on this machine. Restore instead
  2. 2. Write down 24 words, and learn what they do not cover
    The words restore your keys. They do not restore what you hold inside a factory; a second backup does that, and the app keeps it for you. See both backups
  3. 3. Say what you will accept
    Pick the kinds of lobby you accept. That is enough; every other limit starts at a careful default.

    You can see and change every limit later under My conditions.

  4. 4. Join a lobby and wait for it to start
    A lobby is where a host gathers members before a factory starts. Its terms are fixed and signed. When it starts, the app signs for you in seconds if the terms are inside your conditions, and refuses if they are not.

About this concept

Revised after four owner decisions: no host mode and no role question (Hosting always in the menu, one home, one “Needs you” list); buying receiving room moved to a Preview 1.1.0 state; terms as fixed rules with signed ranges that freeze at the ready check, with a rules fingerprint; the 1.0.0 fee kinds.

Console treats Gazpacho as a small node you operate: a side menu with one area per subject, a home made of cards, and a status strip that never leaves. It is the corrected version of the first prototype, which had the same frame but put four scopes into one Settings page, had no Activity and no “my money”, treated conditions as a setting, and promised an absence the specifications do not support.

Avoided on purpose: two entries that sound alike (ThunderHub's Home and Dashboard), technical tools at the top level (Ride The Lightning), more than eight entries (Bisq 2), and Umbrel's habit of hiding detail so that operators install a second console beside it.

Strengths

  • Every subject has one address. A user who asks “where is X” can answer from the menu alone, and the Settings page says where the non-settings went.
  • Health, the next date and Pause are in view on every screen. The most urgent item is never more than a glance away.
  • Several factories, and a host's ladder, have a natural home. The frame grows into a full host console without changing shape.
  • Conditions and terms cannot be confused: different menu entries, different looks (red-edged controls, a blue sealed card), and they meet only in the fit table.
  • Familiar to anyone who has run ThunderHub or Ride The Lightning.
  • Nobody is asked to pick a role. Hosting is one click away, asks nothing until used, and then shares the one home and the one “Needs you” list.

Weaknesses

  • It is a lot of furniture for a new member. Eight entries and five strip items greet someone who only wants to receive a payment, Hosting among them. The wallet-first concepts will feel lighter.
  • At phone width the strip becomes five short lines above every screen, and the menu hides behind a button. Console is a desktop arrangement that tolerates a phone, not a phone design.
  • The wallet scope is split three ways (coins under Pay and receive, keys and backup under Settings, display under App and help). Each placement is defensible; together they are harder to explain than “Wallet”.
  • Watching sits under Settings although it is mostly status. The Home card and the strip carry it, but the “how long can I be away” answer is two clicks deep.
  • “My conditions are a live instruction” is weaker here than in a market layout: they are a destination, not the frame. The Home sentence and the bar above the lobby list do that work.
  • A host with many factories will make the one home long. The hosting section will need a limit, or a link to Hosting in place of detail.

What I would change after seeing it

  • Collapse the strip to two items by default (the worst health state, and the next date), expanding on hover or tap. Five permanent items is the honest maximum, and probably one too many.
  • Try “Watching” folded into the Factories area as “While I am away”, since every number in it comes from a factory. Settings would then hold only wallet, connections and the app.
  • Give the phone a bottom bar of four (Home, Pay, Lobbies, Activity) with the rest under “More”, in place of the drawer.
  • Watch whether an always-visible Hosting entry makes new members think hosting is required. The starting page says it is not; a test would show whether that is enough.
  • Test whether newcomers find “My conditions” before their first join. If not, the first-run step that sets them is carrying too much.
  • Draw the lobby detail for every row, and the carry-over case, which this mock-up only names.
