About this concept

Concept A says: it is a wallet. The home answers "how much can I send, how much can I receive, what could I take out alone" and then gets out of the way. Factories are plumbing. One sentence on the home says what the app will do next and when. Everything about lobbies is one tab away, and the member's conditions sit at the top of that tab.

  • 5 Connections: Settings, Connections, and the first chip of the status strip.

Owner decisions applied (after the first version)

  • No host mode, no role question. The Host tab is always present, with a calm start page until the person hosts something. A hosting section appears on the home only when there is a lobby or factory to show. The Moments menu has an "I also host" switch that fills it with the sample lobby.
  • Buying receiving room is 1.1.0. Its screen, the receiving-room fee term and condition, automatic buying and selling room as a host appear only with "Preview 1.1.0" in the Moments menu, each tagged 1.1.0. The normal view says once: "More receiving room can be bought from version 1.1.0."
  • Fixed outer rules, signed ranges, freeze at the ready check. Sealed cards show the current value and its range, and a rules fingerprint. The fit check compares my limit with the whole range. The looser rule is one condition on the conditions screen. Fennel Yard shows a lobby that fails only because of its range.
  • Fees in 1.0.0. A fee on each payment (base plus proportion) and an optional joining fee (flat, or a share of the chain cost up to a ceiling), taken from the first payment received (open). A fee at each move is open.

Honest strengths

  • Least to learn. Someone who has used any phone wallet knows the home in a second.
  • "My money" is the first thing on screen, with what could be taken out alone directly under the balance, not on a factory page.
  • On an ordinary day the home says one calm sentence and asks for nothing. That is the truth on most days.
  • Conditions and terms are far apart by construction: conditions in their own screens, terms only inside a lobby, meeting only in the fit check and the frozen record.
  • The status strip and Pause never move, so the automation is always one glance and one press away.

Honest weaknesses

  • The three dates are one tap down. The home shows the next date and a factory card, but the full time line, and what happens at each zero, is on the factory screen. A member who never taps the card may not learn that a second and third date exist. The strip can only carry one date.
  • "Open the app by" fits this style worst. A wallet-first home wants to say "nothing to do", yet the open rule says about 1 day unless something watches. The mock-up says both, which reads as a contradiction waiting to happen.
  • The Lobbies tab carries two scopes. Conditions (mine) and terms (theirs) share a tab, kept apart only by look and by a tap. A member can still think "My conditions" is a setting of the lobby list. And conditions are not under Settings, where some will look first; a pointer row there is a patch.
  • Leaving alone and the move have no tab. They are three levels down (Wallet, factory, the move). They surface on the home only when they are happening. That is right for calm days and thin for learning.
  • The full fit check is long on a phone. Fifteen rows in four columns at 360 px is small type. The list shows only "fits" or the failing sentences, so a member can join having read none of the passing rows.
  • The status strip is dense. Four chips and a button in two rows at 360 px costs about 70 px of every screen.
  • A permanent Host tab costs a phone slot. Five labelled tabs at 360 px is the most this layout can carry, and the tab sits there for the many members who will never host.
  • Hosting is squeezed. A host with a ladder wants tables and a wide time line. One phone tab with six rows is workable, not good.
  • Two factories at once has no home on the home. During a move, or with a budget above one, a single balance and a single factory card stop being true.

What I would change after seeing it

  • Put a three-dot mini time line (open by, move by, ends) inside the home's factory card, so all three dates are visible without a tap.
  • Make the home's next-action sentence the same object as the strip's date chip, so the two can never disagree.
  • Collapse the passing rows of the fit check into "13 rows fit: show" on a phone, and keep failing rows always open.
  • On a desktop, give Host a two-pane layout of its own in place of stretched phone cards.
  • Try renaming the Lobbies tab "Join" and moving the frozen terms and the dates under a "Factory" tab once the member is in one; the tab a member needs changes after joining.
  • Decide the open "how long can I be away" question before trusting any wallet-first home: its calm depends on the answer.
