Welcome to Gazpacho

A Lightning wallet where you hold your own keys. You share one on-chain output, called a factory, with a host and other members. The host pays for it. You start with a balance of zero and a channel you can receive into.

  1. Keys made on this machineNobody else has them.
  2. ConnectedTwo chain servers agree at block 330,437. Listings from 4 of 5 relays.
  3. Write down your 24 wordsYou can do this now or after you join. Back up
  4. Join a lobbyThis is the step that makes the wallet work.
Nothing works until you are in a live factory. You cannot pay or receive yet. A host opens a lobby (a waiting room with fixed terms). When enough members are ready, the factory starts and your channel is live. You need no coins to join. A small amount on chain helps if you ever leave alone.

Hosting

Anyone can host, and you can host and be a member at the same time. A host pays for a factory and others join it as members.

What hosting needs

  • Funds on chain for the whole factory. Each member's channel plus your spare liquidity. Example: 30 members with 500,000-sat channels and 250,000 spare each need 22,500,000 sats.
  • A linked Lightning node (LND or Core Lightning). Link a node
  • Being online at the dates your factories set, for every factory you run.

Write terms

Nothing here changes your own channel. Hosting appears on My channel only once you have a lobby or factory of your own.

As a host you pay for a factory and others join it. You write the terms once, sign them, and cannot change them after members are ready.

Hosting

Hosting is always in the menu. There is no switch: anyone can host and be a member at the same time. Nothing about hosting appears on My channel until you have a lobby or factory of your own.

Go to Hosting

What the app always does (no switch anywhere)

  • Runs every safe-to-sign check. Your conditions can only be stricter.
  • Saves before sending, and uses each one-time signing secret once.
  • Compares at least two chain servers and stops when they disagree.
  • Answers an old state, and leaves before the factory ends.
  • Refuses mainnet in this release.
  • Signs once you are ready and the lobby starts. Being ready is the consent; there is no prompt in the middle.

About this concept

Concept E treats a host's lobby as an offer you can compare, and your conditions as a standing instruction the app keeps for you. It borrows the arrangement of peer-to-peer markets and leaves out their trading furniture: there are no price charts, no depth views, no rates and no scores.

Owner decisions applied

  • No role question. The top bar has no member/host switch. Hosting is a normal menu entry with a start page. The Moments menu has a state "I also host" that fills it with the sample lobby; host moments switch it on by themselves.
  • Receiving room is 1.1.0. The buying screen, the automatic-buying condition, the receiving-room fee limit, the lobby's receiving-room fee and the host's sale of room appear only with "Preview 1.1.0" ticked in the Moments menu, each tagged 1.1.0. Normally "can receive" shows the room left and the line "More receiving room can be bought from version 1.1.0."
  • Fixed rules, signed ranges. The sealed card shows a rules fingerprint and says the app signs only for these exact rules. An adjustable value shows "now" and its range, for example the joining fee of Tomato Garden. The fit check compares my limit with the whole range ("fits at any value the host may choose"); one condition offers the looser rule. Sherry Cask shows the difference: its current fee fits, its range does not. The old "new version or new lobby" question is gone: a change of rules means a new lobby.
  • Fees in 1.0.0. Each payment: a base amount plus a proportion. Joining: optional, flat or a share of the chain cost with a ceiling (Olive Press), taken from the first payment received; how it is collected is tagged open. A recurring fee at each move is tagged open.

Strengths

  • Signed ranges fit the offer metaphor well: an offer row shows "now" and "may move", and the safe default compares the whole range, so a late change cannot pull me into something I did not accept.
  • The split between the host's terms and my conditions is the centre of the app. They look different, live in different places, and meet only in the fit check, which is computed from the real form.
  • Automatic joining is one readable sentence with a visible budget, a worst case, a review step and a history. "Nothing fits" comes with the limit that causes it.
  • A lobby that does not fit is never hidden without a reason, and joining by hand is refused by the same limits, so the limits are believable.
  • The host's lasting record is shown where the choice is made, as counts and how each factory ended. Members stay temporary.

Weaknesses

  • The book serves a decision made about once a month. On most days Lobbies and My instruction are quiet, yet they hold the first two places in the menu. Only the landing rule (My channel once joined) hides this.
  • There is no dashboard. Dates and health rely on one urgent line, the footer and My channel. At 360 px the footer line wraps to three lines.
  • My instruction is a long page: limits, three standing permissions and a history. On a phone the sentence is far below the fields that change it.
  • Paying and receiving sit one level under My channel. For a wallet that is one tap too many.
  • The "fits my conditions" switch is a filter driven by the conditions. That is the one spot where the two ideas touch, and a hurried reader may think unticking it loosens something.
  • Hosting is seven sub-pages reached by a row of links. It works, but it is the least considered part.
  • Ranges add a second number to every adjustable row. In the offer book this makes the joining-fee cell long on a phone.

What I would change after seeing it

  • Put Pay and Receive in the top money line, so they are on every screen.
  • Order the menu by state: My channel first once a factory is live, Lobbies first before that.
  • Keep the instruction sentence pinned at the bottom of the form while editing, and on wide screens show the form beside the list of lobbies so the count moves in view.
  • Fold the footer into one tappable summary on phones.
  • Test the word "instruction" with real users against "standing order" and "my rules".
