Welcome to Gazpacho

Gazpacho is a Lightning wallet where you hold your own keys. You share one on-chain output, called a factory, with a host and other members. The host pays for everything. You start with a balance of zero and a channel you can receive into.

Nothing works until you are in a live factory

You cannot pay or receive yet. First you join a lobby: a waiting room a host opens, with fixed terms and a start time. When it starts and confirms, your channel is live.

  1. Keys madeYour 24 recovery words exist on this machine only.
  2. Write the words downBack up now, or later. You will be reminded once money arrives.
  3. Say what you acceptPick profiles below. That is enough to join safely.
  4. Join a lobbyBrowse the list and press Join, or let the app look for you.
  5. Wait for the startThe app signs by itself when the lobby starts. Then you can receive.

What I will accept

These are your conditions. The app joins and signs only inside them. You can change every number later under Lobbies, My conditions.

Every number each profile sets is listed in My conditions.

Three things to know before you start

  • You need no coins to join. A small on-chain amount helps later if you ever leave alone.
  • A factory has dates you must keep. The app shows them on every screen and says what happens at each.
  • How long you can safely be away depends on the factory. For a typical one: about 1 day unless something watches for you. open

Strengths

  • Choosing is honest. Fourteen lobbies on one screen, the same eight facts for each, and a sentence for every one that does not fit. Nothing is hidden to make the market look tidy.
  • The start is legible. A lobby page reads like a game lobby: who is here, one sentence for the start rule, a countdown that says what happens at zero, then a six-step sequence. A failed start says "you did your part" and requeues.
  • Unattended behaviour is never silent. The strip always says a state and a reason, in the same place, and "Looking" explains itself.
  • Terms and conditions cannot be confused. Blue sealed card with a signature bar and no inputs; tomato-edged form in the first person. They meet only in the four-column fit check.
  • Ranges are visible where people choose. The joining fee column shows the range under the value, "Fits" means "fits at any value the host may choose", and a lobby that fits only at today's value gets its own label.

Weaknesses

  • Daily paying and receiving. Once a factory is live, a member wants Pay and Receive and has no use for a lobby list for weeks. Here they are a level below Home, and the app's most developed screen is the one used least. This is the main cost of the style.
  • The strip is about joining. After the start it has little to say, yet it keeps its prime position. The next date had to be squeezed in as a chip. A member's dates deserve more room than a chip.
  • Ranges add weight. Each fee is now a value, a range and a frozen moment. The table carries a second line per fee, and the sealed card grows a fingerprint box.
  • Five tabs at 360 px are tight. Hosting as a peer tab is right by the owner's rule, but it takes a fifth of the phone tab bar for something most members never use.
  • A table is a desktop idea. At 360 px each row becomes a card and the column-to-column comparison that makes a browser useful is mostly lost.
  • My conditions sits under Lobbies. That is right while choosing, and odd later, when conditions also govern the move and automatic buying of receiving room.
  • The factory, the move and leaving alone hang off Home as second-level screens with no tab of their own. A member in two factories during a move would strain this.
  • Two round buttons. Stop-looking and Pause are different things, and two similar icons side by side need their text labels to be understood.

What I would change after seeing it

  • Make the strip change role once a factory is live: lead with the next date and "can send / can receive", and fold joining into a small state at the side.
  • Promote Pay and Receive to buttons in the header or the strip, so they are one tap from anywhere.
  • Consider renaming the Lobbies tab "Factories" after the first join, with the browser as one tab inside it and my live factories first.
  • On a phone, replace the eight-column table with a two-line row and a "compare" view for two or three chosen lobbies.
  • Give Pause words on the button, not an icon.
  • Settle the data conflict this mock-up had to work around: the member is live in factory 12 while browsing, with a budget of one factory at a time. The mock-up assumes the budget counts only automatic joins, and marks that open.
  • The refusal example: the member's own limit is 20 members, the lobby's signed minimum is 30, and the tree offered had 28. The mock-up treats the signed minimum as the limit the app enforces. The wording of "your limit" in that case needs an owner decision.
