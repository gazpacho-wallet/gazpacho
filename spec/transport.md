# Transport (draft 3)

The connection between Gazpacho programs for slice 1: Lightning's encrypted, authenticated connection (BOLT 8) carrying Lightning's message format (BOLT 1). Draft 1 was written 2026-11-11 before the code. Draft 2 (2026-11-12) answers the independent review of pull request 10 (`notes/review-pr10-transport.md`, findings F1 to F10). **Draft 3** (2026-11-12) adds lobby connections: many connections over one poll loop, BOLT 1's `init`, ping and pong, the factory envelope of `messages.md` sections 3 to 5, and events that report every outcome (section "Lobby connections"). Draft 3's additions were reviewed once (reviewer A of pull request 23, `notes/review-pr23-a.md`); the fixes for its findings F1 to F8 are in this draft. Drafts 1 and 2 were reviewed before.

**Reviewed.** The two rounds of that review (`notes/review-pr10-transport.md` and `notes/review-pr10-transport-r2.md`) count as this specification's review under checklist item 1 (decided 2026-11-12). **Exception to item 1:** the specification was reviewed after the code was written, not before; that cannot be repaired for this piece and is recorded here, as is the pull request's size (item 24). Small changes after the second round (findings N1 to N6) are noted where they apply.

## Scope

- Slice 1 only: a member connects directly to the host's address (given to it by hand) and pins the host's key. No Nostr, no address gossip, no reconnection logic (a caller that is told a connection closed connects again).
- Two layers. `conn.c` (drafts 1 and 2) is one blocking connection with no `init` and no ping; `gz-net listen` and `connect` and the BOLT 8 tests use it. `peers.c` (draft 3) is what lobbies use: `init`, ping and pong, the factory envelope, and events.
- The transport lives outside the core, in `ports/net/`. The core never calls it. A program moves bytes between the transport and the core's send actions and message events.

## Pieces

| File | What it is | Input or output |
|---|---|---|
| `ports/net/noise.c` | BOLT 8 state machine: the three acts, then encrypt and decrypt with key rotation | None. Bytes in, bytes out. Randomness comes from a callback (`gz_random_port_t`, the core's own port type) |
| `ports/net/frame.c` | BOLT 1 message framing, BigSize, a strict TLV reader and writer, truncated integers | None |
| `ports/net/conn.c` | POSIX TCP: listen, accept, connect, with timeouts and a size cap, driving the state machine | Sockets and the monotonic clock |
| `ports/net/envelope.c` | The factory envelope (`messages.md` 3 to 5): reading with every record table, writing, splitting and reassembly, `msg_hash`; the bLIP-56 byte mapping (vectors M0, M3; not used in slice 1) | None. The reassembly buffer of one transfer (at most 1 MiB) is the only allocation |
| `ports/net/lnmsg.c` | BOLT 1's `init`, `ping`, `pong`, `warning`, `error` | None |
| `ports/net/peers.c` | Lobby connections: a step-wise handshake for each connection, all driven by one `poll`, `init`, keep-alive, the envelope, events | Sockets and the monotonic clock |
| `tools/gz-net/gz-net.c` | `listen` and `connect`: handshake, then a ping and pong of custom message type 32769. `serve` and `join`: a lobby connection held with pings, `hello` answered by `lobby_status` | The above, and the system's randomness (`getrandom` on Linux, `getentropy` elsewhere; a failure ends the program) |

Cryptography: ECDH from libsecp256k1's ECDH module, whose default hash is SHA-256 of the compressed shared point, exactly BOLT 8's `ECDH`. ChaCha20-Poly1305, SHA-256 and HMAC-SHA256 from OpenSSL's libcrypto, as the imported code already uses. HKDF is the two HMAC steps of RFC 5869 written out (extract, then two blocks of expand with empty `info`), because BOLT 8 gives the intermediate values to check them against.

## What is stored, in what order, crashes

The transport stores nothing. All its state is in memory: handshake hashes and keys, then the two directions' keys, chaining keys and nonces. On a crash, or any error, the connection is gone and a new one starts from a fresh handshake with fresh ephemeral keys. Nothing is resumed. Messages in flight are lost; the core's rule of resending what is saved and not acknowledged (checklist item 16) covers that, not the transport.

The static key a program uses is supplied by its caller. `gz-net` reads it from a file named on the command line, or makes a throwaway one; key storage for real programs belongs to the wallet keys work.

## Rules

1. Acts are exactly 50, 50 and 66 bytes. Any other length is refused.
2. A version byte other than 0 is refused, before anything else in the act is looked at.
3. A public key that does not parse as a compressed secp256k1 point is refused.
4. A failed MAC on any act, length header or message is refused.
5. After any refusal the state is dead: every later call fails, and all keys in it are wiped.
6. Each direction rotates its key when its nonce reaches 1000 (after 500 messages): `ck', k' = HKDF(ck, k)`, nonce back to 0.
7. A message is at most 65,535 bytes. The sender refuses to encrypt more. The receiver reads the length only after its MAC checks, and the connection layer refuses a length above its configured cap (at most 65,535) before reading the body.
8. The member side is always the initiator and is given the host's key. The handshake itself fails if the host does not hold that key: act two's MAC depends on it. That is the pin. The connection layer then compares the handshake's remote key with the pin, but this is a guard that cannot fail while the member is the initiator (the remote key is the pin itself); it has no negative test, and none is possible without a test-only hook.
9. Ephemeral keys come from the randomness callback. A value that is not a valid secret key is drawn again, at most 8 times, then the act fails.
10. Secrets (static and ephemeral secret keys, temporary keys, ECDH outputs, session keys, chaining keys, decrypted bytes of a refused message) are wiped on every exit path. Comparisons of MACs are OpenSSL's constant-time ones; the pinned-key comparison uses `CRYPTO_memcmp` too, though a public key is not secret.
11. Messages: at least 2 bytes (the type). Unknown odd type: ignore. Unknown even type: close.
12. TLV streams: types and lengths minimally encoded BigSize; types strictly increasing; a length past the end fails; a known type is checked by the caller's checker; an unknown even type fails; an unknown odd type is skipped. A writer refuses types out of order and anything past its buffer.
13. The host's side of pinning. A member is identified by its static key, the one its handshake proves it holds; nothing else (an address, a name) identifies it. `gz_conn_accept` requires a member check, called with that key once the handshake is complete and before any message is read; a refusal closes the connection. In slice 1 the host accepts every key (`gz_conn_any_member`), because members are not known in advance: the key a member connects with is the key it joins the lobby with, and from then on the lobby and the factory know the member by that key. A program that knows its members (a host after the lobby closes, or a later slice with invitations) passes a check that compares the key with its list.
14. A state is never copied while in use: two copies would encrypt under the same key and nonces.

## Timeouts and the connection layer

Sockets are non-blocking and close-on-exec, set in the same call that makes them (`SOCK_CLOEXEC | SOCK_NONBLOCK` on `socket`, and `accept4`) where the system has those, so no other thread can start a program between the two; elsewhere `fcntl` sets both just after (N4). Every wait on the network is a `poll` against a deadline from the monotonic clock, and a `poll`, `recv` or `send` interrupted by a signal is resumed. Addresses must be numeric (`AI_NUMERICHOST`): no name is looked up, because name resolution could not be bounded by a deadline. A name is refused with its own error, "address must be numeric" (N5). Waiting for a connection has its own limit (`accept_ms`; none by default). The handshake must finish within the handshake timeout (default 10 s), counted from the connection. A message, header and body together, must arrive whole within the receive timeout (default 30 s) from when its first byte is awaited, so a peer that trickles bytes is cut off. A write that cannot finish within the send timeout fails.

A peer's close never raises SIGPIPE: writes use `MSG_NOSIGNAL` where the system has it, otherwise each socket gets `SO_NOSIGPIPE`; a system with neither does not build `conn.c` (it stops with an error rather than fall back silently). The tests run with SIGPIPE at its default action.

## Lobby connections (draft 3)

A lobby gathers for hours (`messages.md` section 2), so a host keeps many members connected and idle. `ports/net/peers.c` does this; `conn.c` is unchanged.

L1. **One loop.** Every connection, listening socket and timer is served by one `poll`. The BOLT 8 acts are read and written step by step as bytes arrive, so a peer that is silent or slow in its handshake holds only its own slot until its deadline, and every other member is served meanwhile. This settles review finding F2 in favour of the step-wise handshake (no threads).

L2. **Deadlines.** For a connection we make, TCP connect, the three acts and the peer's `init` must all be done within `handshake_ms` (10 s) of the call; for one we accept, within `handshake_ms` of the accept. Once a message has started (its first header byte read), the whole message must arrive within `recv_ms` (30 s): a peer that trickles bytes is closed. Between messages there is no deadline: an idle connection is kept by L4, not cut. Queued bytes that make no progress for `send_ms` (10 s) close the connection.

L3. **`init`.** Each side sends `init` as soon as its handshake is done: no global features, no features (a build without discovery sets no bit, `messages.md` section 2), and `networks` (TLV 1) holding exactly the chain hash the program is configured for, signet in slice 1. The peer's first message must be `init`; its features must hold no even bit (this program knows no feature, so every even bit is an unknown required one, BOLT 1); its `networks` must be exactly our chain; `remote_addr` and other odd records are skipped. Anything else is refused, and because BOLT 1 says to send nothing but `init` until the peer's `init` has been received, a refusal before a valid `init` has arrived closes **without a warning**. Nothing but `init` can be sent before the peer's `init` has been accepted. A second `init` is refused.

L4. **Ping and pong.** After `ping_ms` (15 s) without a whole message from the peer, a side sends `ping` with `num_pong_bytes` cycling through 1 to 16 and no ignored bytes, and waits for its `pong`; at most one ping is outstanding. A `pong` whose length is not the one asked for is refused; a `pong` not asked for is ignored. No `pong` within `pong_ms` (30 s) closes the connection. A received `ping` is answered with exactly `num_pong_bytes` zero bytes, unless it asks for 65,532 or more, which BOLT 1 says to ignore, or the peer is not reading (its queue is full). More than `max_pings` (10) pings in one `pong_ms` window are refused. **Worst case:** a peer that dies is noticed 45 s after its last message (15 s to the ping, 30 s for the pong). Against the shortest step timeout of 60 s (`messages.md` section 2) that leaves 15 s to reconnect, handshake (deadline 10 s), exchange `init` and send `hello`: enough on a working link, tight on a slow one. A peer that sends its own pings more often than every 15 s is never pinged, because it is never silent; its traffic shows the path is alive.

L5. **Refusing.** "Refuse" (`messages.md` section 1) is: stop reading, queue a `warning` with an all-zero `channel_id` and a short ASCII reason (only once the peer's valid `init` has arrived, L3), close once it is written, when the peer hangs up, or after `send_ms`. A received `error` closes at once. A received `warning` is reported as a `WARNING` event, the first `max_warning_events` (10) per connection; later ones are only counted. Messages that change nothing (unknown odd message types, unknown odd and discovery kinds, a wrong protocol tag, warnings) are counted, and more than `max_ignored` (100) in one `pong_ms` window are refused. An unknown even type is refused.

L6. **Factory messages.** Type 52791 is read with `envelope.c` as `messages.md` sections 3 to 5 say: the receiving side is the host on connections it accepted and the member on connections it made, which decides the direction rule. A wrong protocol tag is ignored, with one `warning` per connection. Parts are put back together per connection, one transfer at a time, at most 1 MiB; an incomplete transfer is dropped with its connection. Only the host splits: `gz_peers_send` refuses records over 65,495 bytes on a connection it made.

L7. **Events.** `gz_peers_poll` returns one event at a time: `CONNECTED` (handshake and `init` done), `MESSAGE` (a whole factory message, after reassembly), `WARNING`, `CLOSED`. Each names the connection (an id never reused) and, once the handshake has proved it, the peer's node id (its static key; for a connection we make, the pin). `CLOSED` says why: lost, refused, a timeout (connect, handshake, pong, message, send), the handshake, our member check, `init`, our refusal of a message (with the reason), the peer's `error` (with its text), decryption, a socket error, our own `gz_peers_refuse`, an eviction, or a full host (L9). A connection closed with `gz_peers_close` gives no event, and events already queued for it are dropped. Nothing fails silently.

L8. **Keys.** A connection we make may use its own static key (`messages.md` section 2: a fresh key per new connection, or the seat's key); otherwise the configured one. The host's member check (rule 13) is required, as for `conn.c`.

L9. **Admission and bounds** (review of PR 23, F1). Accepted connections in their handshake (no valid `init` yet) have their own budget, `max_handshakes` (16); when it is full, the **oldest** of them is evicted to make room, so silent sockets cannot keep members out: an honest handshake takes milliseconds. At most `max_per_address` (4) accepted connections come from one IP address; more are closed at accept. At most `max_conns` (64) connections are established. When a newcomer's `init` arrives and they are all taken, the oldest accepted connection **without a seat** is evicted; a seat is marked by the lobby engine with `gz_peers_set_seated` at `join` and cleared at `leave`. If every accepted connection holds a seat, the newcomer is refused ("host full"). Evictions and refusals are counted in `gz_peers_stats` and reported as `CLOSED` events where a connection existed. At most `max_queue` (4 MiB) unsent bytes per connection: a send that would exceed it returns "full" and keeps the connection. A completed transfer's buffer is wiped and freed as soon as its message is delivered; the buffer of each decrypted message, the buffer of each outgoing factory message, reassembly buffers and event copies are wiped before they are freed or reused. Running out of memory ends the program (an event must never be lost silently).

## Limits of this cut

- `conn.c`'s accept and handshake are one blocking call (review finding F2). Lobbies use `peers.c` instead (L1), so this now limits only `gz-net listen` and the tests that use `conn.c`.
- In `conn.c` the idle timeout and the per-message deadline are still the same timeout: a `conn.c` connection idle for 30 s is cut off. `peers.c` splits the two (L2, L4).
- `peers.c` connects to the first address a numeric host gives; it does not try the others.
- **Established hold** (review of PR 23, F1). The transport evicts only connections without a seat, and only when the host is full. A host therefore depends on the lobby engine to call `gz_peers_set_seated` at `join` and to close a connection that has sent neither `hello` nor `join` within a limit it chooses (for example 60 s), and to cap connections without a seat. Recorded for the lobby stream (S4). Until then, members that hold no seat can fill the slots only until a newcomer evicts the oldest of them; seated members are never evicted. The per-address cap also limits members behind one shared address (a household, a carrier NAT) to 4.
- POSIX only. Windows needs its own `conn` and `peers` behind the same headers (and `fd` as an opaque handle).
- The randomness port still cannot report a failure (review finding F7, decision 12); `gz-net` ends the program instead. Changing the port belongs to the core.

## Decisions where BOLT 8, BOLT 1 and our documents are silent

All twelve were confirmed by the review of PR 10 (decisions 1 to 10 in round 1, 11 and 12 in round 2). The one open design choice is under "Limits of this cut".

1. Every refusal is final for the state, including the caller's own mistakes (a message over 65,535 bytes, a too-small output buffer, a call out of order). Simpler to reason about than a mix of fatal and recoverable errors. `gz_noise_remote_static` is a query, not a refusal: before the handshake ends it answers "not yet" and changes nothing.
2. Order of checks in an act: length, then version, then key, then MAC, matching the order of the BOLT 8 failure vectors.
3. Ephemeral keys: up to 8 draws from the randomness port before giving up.
4. HKDF and HMAC are written out over OpenSSL's one-shot HMAC; ChaCha20-Poly1305 and SHA-256 are OpenSSL's EVP, as in the imported `crypto_aead.c`. The imported code itself is not called, so the transport does not link the SuperScalar library.
5. ECDH uses libsecp256k1's ECDH module (already enabled in the pinned build) rather than a hand-written multiply and hash.
6. Keys after the handshake: the chaining key, temporary key, ephemeral secret and our static secret are wiped as soon as act three is done; the session keys and the two public keys stay.
7. The connection layer's receive timeout covers one whole message from when its first byte is awaited, so an idle connection also times out (30 s by default). Keep-alive belongs to the caller (BOLT 1's ping and pong are not in this cut).
8. The member is always the initiator and the host always the responder, so the pin is the handshake's own `rs`. The explicit comparison after act two cannot fail while that holds; it is kept as a guard against a later change, and labelled as such in the code (rule 8).
9. `gz-net`'s custom message 32769 carries a TLV stream (type 1: kind, 1 ping or 2 pong; type 3: sequence number, tu64). Odd, so other software ignores it.
10. A static key is read from a file of exactly 64 hex digits and an optional line end, never from the command line, so it does not appear in process listings. Pins, ports and counts are parsed strictly too: anything else in them is refused.
11. A `gz_conn_t` starts zeroed and is reusable after it is closed. Accept or connect on one that is still open is refused and leaves it working (it would otherwise leak its socket). Closing a zeroed one closes no descriptor.
12. The randomness port cannot report a failure, so `gz-net` ends the program if the system's randomness cannot be read rather than continue with weak values. It reads with `getrandom` (or `getentropy`), so no buffer of future key bytes stays in the process (N3). Letting the core's port report failures is left to the core.

Draft 3 adds these, where BOLT 1 and `messages.md` leave a choice (not yet reviewed):

13. "Silence" before a ping means no whole message received from the peer; what we send does not count. So when both sides are idle, whichever timer runs out first pings, and the other side, having just heard from it, usually does not.
14. A `pong` must have exactly the length the outstanding ping asked for, or the connection is refused (BOLT 1 allows failing it). A `pong` with no ping outstanding is ignored.
15. Ping lengths cycle through 1 to 16, so a late `pong` cannot pass for the current one; no ignored bytes are sent.
16. The ping flood limit is more than 10 pings in one `pong_ms` window, configurable. BOLT 1 gives no number; its rationale says only that "limited precautions are recommended against `ping` flooding". `gz-net` raises the limit only when its ping interval is shorter than 15 s (its test settings), to 10 plus three times its own ping rate; at the real values it uses 10.
17. The peer's `init` must arrive within the handshake deadline. A message other than `init` first is refused with the reason "first message is not init"; a second `init` is refused.
18. `networks` must be present and hold exactly one chain hash, ours. Missing `networks` is refused like a wrong one.
19. A warning before closing is sent for every refusal once the peer's valid `init` has arrived. Before that (the first message is not `init`, or `init` is malformed, names another chain or requires an unknown feature) the connection is closed without one, as BOLT 1 requires. There is none for handshake failures either (nothing can be encrypted yet) and none for the member check (rule 13: closed at once).
20. Kind 2 (`part`) is treated as host to member only: a host refuses a part as a kind in the wrong direction (`messages.md` 5: "members send no parts in slice 1").
21. A part whose `transfer_id` is the open transfer's but whose `inner_kind`, `total_len`, `count` or `scope` differ is refused. A transfer whose whole message would have fitted one envelope (65,001 to 65,495 bytes) is accepted: the receiving rules of section 5 do not refuse it, though a sender never makes one.
22. Records whose encoding `messages.md` leaves open (`evidence`, `all_partials`, `payouts`, `penalties`) are skipped as unknown odd records; `distribution` (44) is refused as an unknown even record, as 7.2 says for slice 1.
23. `round_all_nonces` items are checked for shape only: whole blocks, 1 to 256 items, at least one signer each. Whether they match the round is the signing engine's check. Likewise every other semantic check (signatures, versions, seat rules) is above the transport; `gz_peers_refuse` lets the engine refuse.
24. "ASCII" (`round_abort`'s `detail`, warning texts) means printable ASCII, 0x20 to 0x7e. `display_name`'s UTF-8 must be well formed (RFC 3629: no overlong forms, no surrogates, nothing above U+10FFFF).
25. This build has no discovery, so every discovery kind (128 to 159) is ignored, whatever it holds. A reassembled message of an unknown odd kind is ignored.
26. Defaults: 64 established connections, 16 handshakes in progress, 4 connections per address, 4 MiB of unsent bytes per connection, 100 ignored messages per window, 10 warning events per connection. A send over the queue limit returns an error instead of closing. Evictions (the oldest handshake; the oldest connection without a seat) give `CLOSED` events; an accept refused for its address gives none, only a counter (review of PR 23, F1).

## Tests, listed before the code

BOLT 8 vectors, byte for byte: initiator success (act one, act three, the intermediate `h`, `ck` and temporary keys, and the final `sk`, `rk`); initiator act two: short read, bad version, bad key, bad MAC; responder success (act two, `rk`, `sk`, the recovered `rs`); responder act one: short read, bad version, bad key, bad MAC; responder act three: bad version, short read, bad ciphertext MAC, bad `rs`, bad MAC; message encryption outputs 0, 1, 500, 501, 1000, 1001 and both rotation HKDF outputs, and the receiver decrypting all 1002.

BOLT 1 vectors: BigSize decoding (18) and encoding (8); TLV decoding failures, successes and stream failures from Appendix B, with the n1 and n2 namespaces written in the test; Appendix C's `init` extensions; appending each invalid stream to each valid stream fails; appending a higher-numbered valid stream to a lower one succeeds.

Hostile and boundary cases: acts one byte short and one byte long; calls out of order; every call after a failure; bad MAC on a length header and on a body; body given with the wrong length; a 65,536-byte message refused by the sender; a length above the cap refused by the connection before the body is read; rotation exactly at message 500 and not 499, in both directions; a randomness callback that gives only invalid keys; keys wiped after success and after failure.

Connection, between local processes: handshake and ping-pong; a wrong pin refused with nothing sent after act one; a silent peer timed out; a truncated act; a peer that closes mid-message; an oversize length from an authenticated peer.

Added in draft 2 (review of PR 10): both sides sending 600 messages before either reads, so the two directions rotate in a different order (F1); every call in every handshake phase refused; the member check seeing the key and its refusal closing the connection; the handshake deadline starting at the connection; a member facing a silent host; the body sharing the message's deadline; SIGPIPE at its default with a closed peer; a slow reader getting twenty full-size messages and a reader that never reads cut off by the send deadline; a signal every 5 ms during a slow message; reuse of an open connection; close-on-exec; names refused; `gz-net` closing on an unknown even message, ignoring an unknown odd one, refusing a wrong pong, and refusing loose keys, pins and numbers; every wipe of a stack secret counted (the transport wipes through one function, `gz_net_wipe` in `ports/net/wipe.c`, which the test wraps; wrapping `OPENSSL_cleanse` itself would also catch a static libcrypto's own wipes, as the MemorySanitizer build showed), and HMAC made to fail at each of its calls in a handshake and in a rotation. Opt-in (`-DGZ_INTEROP_PY=ON`, needs Python's `cryptography`): `gz-net` against the reviewer's independent Python BOLT 8 peer, as host and as member, 1,200 pings each way (past rotation), so two copies of this code are not each other's only judge. **It should run in the automated checks** (N6): the CI hardening work is to turn it on wherever Python's `cryptography` is installed, including before every merge.

Added after round 2 (N1, N2, N5): the wipe test checks which buffer each wipe clears, not only how many bytes, by comparing the wiped bytes with values from the BOLT 8 vectors (every ECDH output, chaining key, temporary key and session key, each HKDF's PRK and its T block, and the handshake secrets); a key file ending in CRLF reads as the same key; a name is refused as "address must be numeric" by the library and by `gz-net`; send, receive and remote_static on a never-opened connection say it is closed; the member's, host's and listening sockets are checked to be close-on-exec and non-blocking.

A randomised TLV test against an independent model: a generator that builds streams record by record and knows whether it broke one, compared with the parser's verdict and the records it returns, in order.

Fuzz targets, written for libFuzzer and also built with a plain driver for the build server (which has no clang): the decrypting reader, and the TLV parser.

Mutation run: a list of deliberate small faults in `ports/net/`, each built and tested in a scratch copy, reporting which no test catches.

Added in draft 3: vectors M0 to M3 of `messages.md` byte for byte (M1's signature verified, M3 to M1 and back); every kind's record table, written in the test from the specification, at every limit (each required record missing, each size one byte short and long, each list one byte off and one entry past its bounds, an unknown even and odd record, a record after 240, a zero scope, the wrong direction); kinds, tags, discovery and the largest 65,535-byte message; splitting at the thresholds and at 1 MiB, in order, reversed and scrambled, and every hostile part of `messages.md` tests 9 to 14; `init`, ping, pong, warning and error, malformed at every field; between processes, a host and members over one loop: messages both ways with node ids, a 1 MiB message in parts, a fresh key per connection, an idle connection kept by pings far past the old idle cut, a silent connection not delaying another member's handshake, and each failure reported as its event (refused, handshake and pong timeouts, wrong pin, other chain, member check, connection limit, a message never finished, a peer that never reads, a pong of the wrong length, `init` not first, refusals with their warnings, a peer's error); `gz-net serve` and `join` holding a connection for 4 s at a 100 ms ping (six minutes at 15 s with `-DGZ_LONG_TESTS=ON`); the Python peer speaking `init`, ping, pong, `hello` and `lobby_status` against `gz-net` in both roles; a fuzz target `fuzz_envelope`; and mutants for the three new files.
